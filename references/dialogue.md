# Dialogue

Good dialogue reveals people, moves the plot and creates conflict. Each line must do at least one job: move the plot, reveal character, create conflict, pass on information, express feeling, or raise a question.

## Dialogue tells (rewrite on sight)

- **The mirrored rebuttal.** "You're terrible." "I'm not terrible, I'm precise." One character relabels the accusation. It is the not-X-but-Y pattern in dialogue form, and AI fiction repeats it constantly.
- **Quotable lines.** Every character speaks in aphorisms. Real people rarely land a perfect line.
- **Ping-pong symmetry.** Each reply mirrors the length and shape of the last one.
- **Info dumps.** "As you know, the Corporation was founded in 2090..." Characters don't tell each other what both already know.
- **Over-politeness.** "I'm sorry to bother you, but could you possibly..." becomes "Hey. Hand me that."
- **Speeches.** A character delivers a philosophy lecture. Show the view through conflict and action.
- **Empty small talk.** Weather and lunch talk, unless it reveals character or hides something.

## Be short

People under pressure drop words.

| Wordy | Short |
|---|---|
| "What I want to tell you is that I think we should leave right now." | "We have to go." |
| "I'm so sorry, but I honestly don't know the answer to what you just asked." | "I don't know." |

## Real speech is incomplete

It interrupts, hesitates, changes topic, trails off and implies.

> "I was going to tell you, but..."
> "But what?"
> "Forget it."
> "No. Say it."
> "It's nothing."

## Subtext

The real meaning sits under the surface.

| Direct | With subtext |
|---|---|
| "I'm angry." | "I'm fine. Really." |
| "I like you." | "You look... okay today." |
| "I don't trust you." | "Thanks for telling me. I'll remember it." |
| "I want to leave." | "The air in here is bad." |

Techniques: change the subject, answer with a question, talk about something else, answer with silence and an action.

## Tags and beats

- Leave out the tag when the speaker is clear.
- Use neutral tags: "said", "asked".
- Don't use adverb tags. Not "she said angrily" but "Her voice shook." or an action.
- An action beat can replace a tag: *"You're lying." She slammed the table.*
- Matching action strengthens a line. Contradicting action reveals the truth: *"I support you completely." He looked away, his fist tight in his pocket.*
- Start a new paragraph for each speaker.

## Voices

Every character should be recognizable without tags. Vary:

- Words: formal, slang, dialect, jargon.
- Sentence length: long, short, broken.
- Pauses: fluent, hesitant, interrupting.
- Tone: gentle, harsh, sarcastic, flat.
- A verbal habit.

## Scene types

- **Argument.** Short lines, interruptions, repetition, escalation.
- **Confession.** Hesitation, pauses, searching for words.
- **Interrogation.** Questions control the scene; information is unequal; pressure builds.
- **Flirting.** Double meanings, testing, closeness.
- **Combat.** Dry and short.
- **Relationship.** Expansive and warm.

## Checklist

- Does each line have a job? Would cutting it hurt the scene?
- Can you tell who speaks without tags?
- Is there subtext?
- Is the pace right for the scene?
- Does it fit the character?
- No mirrored rebuttals, no info dumps, no speeches.
