# Characters

Readers remember people, not plots.

## Protagonists

Each needs a clear goal, a strong motive, something the reader can feel with, and room to change. A fatal flaw must cause real failure at key moments.

| Flaw | Failure it causes |
|---|---|
| Arrogance | Underestimates the enemy and walks into a trap |
| Distrust | Refuses help and ends up alone |
| Perfectionism | Can't act in time |
| Revenge | Gets used and loses judgment |

## Antagonists

Strong and credible. They believe they are right, have a real connection to the protagonist, and challenge the protagonist's beliefs. "I want to destroy the world" is flat. "I lost everything, so the world means nothing" is understandable and twisted.

## Supporting cast

Every supporting character has a job: mentor, ally, comic relief, love interest, traitor. Cut anyone who only exists. Avoid stereotypes unless they are a deliberate choice.

## Depth

- **Contradiction.** The gang enforcer who feeds stray cats. The detective afraid of the dark.
- **Show traits through behavior.** Not "he was angry" but "he crushed the paper cup". Not "she was kind" but "she had been feeding the stray dog for three years".
- **Voice.** See `dialogue.md`.
- **Motive.** Every action has a reason, even a twisted one.
- **Out of character.** A character acting against type needs a reason, given in the same or the next chapter. It can mark growth.

## Relationships

Relationships change over the story: strangers, reluctant allies, trust, betrayal or test, then real friendship or a break. Reveal depth in layers: surface behavior, shared experience, then true feelings and secrets.

## First appearances

Introduce a character in action, in conflict or in a misunderstanding.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "Li Ming, 28, was a detective. He walked into the office." | Li Ming stepped over the tape while an officer tried to stop him. "Homicide." He showed his badge and went straight to the body. |
| "A beautiful girl named Hong sat by the window." | She hadn't slept in three days, and the coffee shook in her cup. When the doorbell rang she nearly dropped it. |

Never use a mirror description or a résumé line.

## Consistency questions

- Does this fit the character's core value?
- Given their background, would they react this way?
- How does their fear shape this decision?
- What mistake would their flaw lead them to?
